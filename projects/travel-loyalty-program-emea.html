Loyalty Programme, Multi-Market Scale

Marketing Strategist and Programme Lead · Salesforce CRM, Google Analytics, Excel · Travel and Hospitality

The Situation

A loyalty programme layered onto a campaign already in motion.

Midway through the planning for a major multi-region campaign, the brief changed. A loyalty programme component was being added — not as a separate initiative, but as an integrated layer of the same campaign, running across the same three markets: EMEA, Europe, and South America.

This was not a simple addition. A loyalty programme is a different kind of promise to an audience than a campaign impression. It creates expectations around continuity, reward, and brand relationship that a one-off campaign does not. Bolting it onto an existing campaign brief without rethinking the strategy would have produced something inconsistent — and potentially damaging to the brand relationship the campaign was trying to build.

Integrating Without Disrupting

Making the loyalty layer feel native, not added on.

I went back to the campaign framework and rebuilt the audience journey to accommodate two tracks — one for new audiences encountering the brand for the first time through the campaign, and one for audiences who were being invited into a longer-term relationship through the loyalty programme. The messaging had to work for both without feeling like two different campaigns stitched together.

Operationally, this meant building a separate but connected delivery workflow. The campaign assets and the loyalty enrolment assets needed to coordinate at the channel level, hit different KPIs, and report against different success metrics — all while sharing the same creative direction and brand language. I mapped the dependencies between the two workstreams, defined the handoff points, and made sure neither track was waiting on the other to move.

The loyalty programme launch was integrated into the campaign timeline without pushing the overall launch date — achieved by running the two workstreams in parallel with clearly defined sync points rather than sequentially.

What It Demanded

Holding two strategies in the same hand.

The complexity of this project was not in either workstream individually. It was in managing the relationship between them — making sure the campaign was building the kind of awareness that made the loyalty programme feel like a natural next step, and making sure the loyalty enrolment experience was delivering on the promise the campaign was making.

There were moments where the two tracks pulled in different directions — a message that worked brilliantly for campaign awareness was too transactional for loyalty enrolment. Every one of those tensions had to be identified and resolved before assets went into production, because once they were live across three markets, there was no pulling them back.

"A loyalty programme is not a campaign. It is a relationship with a deadline attached. The strategy has to account for both."

What Came Of It

The programme landed. The campaign exceeded target. Both on time.

Integrated Loyalty programme launched in tandem with the campaign across all three regions without schedule slippage
117% Of combined campaign and programme targets achieved — the loyalty layer contributed to, not competed with, the overall result
Zero audience confusion Unified brand voice maintained across campaign and loyalty communications in EMEA, EU, and South America

When the results came in at 117 percent of the overall target, the loyalty programme was not a footnote. It was part of what got us there — because it had been treated as part of the strategy from the beginning, not an addition to an already-closed brief.

The best loyalty programmes do not feel like programmes. They feel like the brand remembering who you are — which only happens when the people building them treat it as a relationship problem, not a marketing mechanics problem.
