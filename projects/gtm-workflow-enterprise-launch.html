GTM Workflow, Enterprise Product Launch

GTM Lead & Program Manager · Salesforce, Asana, Excel, PowerPoint · B2B Enterprise

The Situation

A product ready to ship. A go-to-market that was not.

The product had been in development for months. The launch date was already on the calendar. What did not exist yet was a functioning go-to-market workflow — no clear owner, no briefing structure, no agreement between product, marketing, legal, and the regional delivery teams on what was actually being launched, to whom, and by when.

I came in at the point where the energy was high and the clarity was not. Everyone had a version of the launch in their head. Nobody had written it down in a way the whole team could operate from.

Building the Framework

The GTM brief as the source of truth.

The first thing I built was the GTM brief — not a deck, not a meeting, a document. A single source of truth that named the product, the audience, the message hierarchy, the channel plan, the regional rollout sequence, and the ownership of every deliverable. I wrote the first draft fast and put it in front of the team not because it was right, but because a bad draft generates better feedback than a blank page.

That document became the backbone of everything that followed. Every briefing to creative teams, every status update to leadership, every risk flag to legal — all anchored to the same framework so nothing could drift without someone catching it.

The brief was written, reviewed, and signed off across four cross-functional teams in under two weeks — before a single asset went into production.

The Turning Point

When scope changed and the brief held anyway.

Midway through the campaign build, one of the regional teams surfaced a compliance requirement that had not been factored in. In most setups, that would have triggered a cascade of rework and a delayed launch. Because the GTM brief had been structured with clear acceptance criteria and defined scope boundaries, we could absorb the change without unravelling the whole plan. We negotiated a regional content variant, updated the brief, and the launch timeline held.

"The real value of a GTM framework is not what it does when everything goes to plan. It is what it absorbs when things do not."

What Came Of It

On time. On message. No surprises.

100% On-schedule delivery across all regional markets, with zero launch-day escalations
4 teams Product, marketing, legal, and regional delivery aligned to a single GTM brief throughout
Zero rework On core messaging and creative assets — scope changes absorbed at the brief level, not the production level

The launch went live across all target markets on schedule. More importantly, the process itself became the reference model for the next product launch on the same account — which is the outcome I am most proud of.

A go-to-market workflow is not a project plan. It is a shared understanding of what winning looks like, written down early enough that everyone has time to disagree before it is too late to change anything.
