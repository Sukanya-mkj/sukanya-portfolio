Assistant Secretary, Cultural and Arts Society

Event Coordination and Creative Programming · St. Xavier's University, Kolkata

The Role

Where coordination instincts first got tested.

Before programme management was a job title, it was a habit of mind — a tendency to look at a group of people trying to do something together and immediately start thinking about how to make it work. The Cultural and Arts Society at St. Xavier's University was where that instinct first had somewhere to go.

As Assistant Secretary, I was responsible for the operational side of the society's event calendar: coordinating between student volunteers, venue administration, faculty advisors, and external performers. The events ranged from intimate literary evenings to large-scale cultural fests drawing students from across the university. None of them ran themselves.

What the Work Actually Involved

Logistics, people, and last-minute pivots.

Every event started with a plan and ended differently. Performers cancelled. Rooms changed. Volunteers committed and then disappeared. What I learned was that the plan itself was never the point — the point was having enough clarity about the goal that you could adapt the plan without losing the outcome.

I managed vendor coordination for staging and audio, maintained communication with participants and performers across multiple events simultaneously, and kept the Secretary and faculty advisor informed without pulling them into decisions that didn't need them. It was a small operation, but it had most of the moving parts that larger programmes have: multiple stakeholders, real deadlines, and no one person responsible for everything except the person whose job it was to hold it together.

What It Left Behind

An early lesson in what programme management actually is.

Most of what I now do professionally — holding a brief, managing upward, keeping a team pointed at the same goal — I first did badly at a university cultural festival and then got slightly better at the next one. The stakes were lower. The feedback was faster. The habits were forming.

If there is one thing the role made clear early, it is that the quality of an event is determined mostly before it happens. The night itself is just the execution of decisions already made. Getting those decisions right, and getting them made by the right people, is the actual work.

"The best events feel effortless. The effort is just invisible by the time anyone arrives."
