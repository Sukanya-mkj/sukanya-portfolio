Enterprise Product Launch & AI-Augmented GTM Workflow

GTM Lead & Program Manager · Salesforce, Asana, HubSpot, Claude, Microsoft Copilot · Fortune 500 / B2B Enterprise

The Situation

A product ready to ship. A go-to-market that was not.

The product had been in development for months. The launch date was already on the calendar. What did not exist yet was a functioning go-to-market workflow. No clear owner, no briefing structure, no agreement between product, marketing, legal, and the regional delivery teams on what was actually being launched, to whom, and by when.

Underneath the launch itself sat a second problem. The GTM workflow was slow in ways nobody had stopped to question. BRDs that took three days to draft because they started from scratch every time. Status reports assembled manually from five different sources. Campaign briefs requiring four rounds of editing before they were usable. I came in to run the program thinking: none of this needs to be this slow.

Building the Framework

The GTM brief as the source of truth. Then making it faster.

The first thing I built was the GTM brief. Not a deck, not a meeting. Just a document. A single source of truth that named the product, the audience, the message hierarchy, the channel plan, the regional rollout sequence, and the ownership of every deliverable. I wrote the first draft fast and put it in front of the team because a bad draft generates better feedback than a blank page. That document became the backbone of everything that followed: every briefing to creative teams, every status update to leadership, every risk flag to legal.

Then I turned to the overhead. I embedded Claude and Copilot into the BRD authoring process, built prompt frameworks that produced a working first draft from a structured intake form, and restructured HubSpot workflows so campaign status updated automatically at key handoff points. The goal: remove the mechanical work so the team could spend their hours on the work that actually required them.

The GTM brief was signed off across four cross-functional teams in under two weeks, before a single asset went into production. Documentation turnaround dropped by roughly 40 percent once the AI-augmented workflow was embedded.

The Turning Points

When scope changed, and when the team had to trust a process they did not build.

Midway through the campaign build, a regional compliance requirement surfaced that had not been factored in. Because the GTM brief had defined scope boundaries clearly, we absorbed the change without unravelling the plan. We negotiated a regional content variant, updated the brief, and the launch timeline held. Zero rework on core messaging or creative assets.

The harder challenge was the team's relationship with the new workflow. Introducing AI raised real questions. Not about whether the tools worked, but about what it meant for the work they were valued for. I was deliberate about this. Clear about what the AI was not doing: not replacing judgment, not signing off on anything, not lowering the quality bar. Over time, the resistance gave way to genuine adoption once the reclaimed hours went toward higher-impact work.

"The real value of a GTM framework is not what it does when everything goes to plan. It is what it absorbs when things do not."

What Came Of It

On time. On message. A workflow the team kept.

100% On-schedule delivery across all regional markets, zero launch-day escalations
~40% Reduction in documentation turnaround across BRDs, briefs, and stakeholder reports
5+ hrs/week Reclaimed from manual reporting, redirected into strategic review and client communication
Full adoption AI-augmented workflow became the team standard and the reference model for the next product launch on the same account

The launch went live on schedule. The process became the reference model for the next product launch on the same account. The more lasting outcome was a workflow the team owned, understood, and continued to run after the engagement closed. That is the version of GTM transformation I am most proud of.

A go-to-market workflow is not a project plan. It is a shared understanding of what winning looks like, written down early enough that everyone can disagree. And built fast enough that the machine can handle the rest.
