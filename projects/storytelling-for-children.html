Case 08

Storytelling for Children, Creating With Impact

Illustrator & Narrative Contributor · Pencil, paper and crayons · CSR, Volunteer Creative

The Situation

An NGO wanted a culturally inclusive illustrated book to support early childhood literacy, and the project relied entirely on volunteers coordinating across time zones with no formal production process. Everyone involved was generous with their time, but nobody had a shared plan for how the book would actually come together.

Stepping In

I contributed as an illustrator and narrative voice, developing visual storytelling sequences aligned to different reading levels. I helped refine the tone and layout so the story worked for the children who would eventually hold it, not just for the volunteers designing it from the outside.

"The book was not for us. Every choice had to work for a child holding it for the first time."
The Turning Point

The most meaningful moment was realizing that the simplest version of a page, not the most detailed one, usually worked best for early readers. Letting go of complexity was harder than adding it, especially when every contributor wanted their favorite detail to stay in.

What Came Of It

The book reached underserved communities through the NGO's distribution channels and was adopted into structured literacy sessions, by building a narrative and visual system simple enough for volunteer teams to execute consistently without a formal production budget behind them. The project was later featured in the NGO's annual literacy impact report.

The simplest stories often carry the deepest weight, and storytelling remains one of the most powerful interventions in early education.
