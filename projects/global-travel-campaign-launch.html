Global Travel Campaign Launch

End-to-End Marketing Strategist & Campaign Lead · Salesforce, Google Analytics, Asana · Travel & Hospitality

The Situation

Three regions. One campaign. No unified playbook.

The client was a global travel and hospitality brand with a presence across EMEA, Europe, and South America. They had a campaign to launch. What they did not have was a strategy that could hold across three regions with different audience behaviours, different regulatory landscapes, different languages, and very different relationships with the brand.

I was handed the brief and the target — and the end-to-end ownership of getting from one to the other. No campaign manager above me, no separate strategy team. Four months, three regions, and a number to hit.

Building the Strategy

One campaign framework. Three regional expressions.

The first decision I made was that a single campaign could not mean a single execution. The brand message needed to be consistent. The way it landed in each region needed to be different — shaped around local audience motivations, local channel performance data, and local market timing.

I built a campaign framework that held the core message fixed and gave the regional teams a structured brief they could adapt within defined parameters. This meant the campaign spoke with one voice while still feeling native to each market. It also meant regional teams were not reinventing the strategy — they were executing a version of it they understood and owned.

Alongside the framework, I built the full-funnel reporting infrastructure: KPI definitions aligned to the campaign goal, tracking set up across channels before a single asset went live, and a weekly reporting cadence that gave leadership real-time visibility without requiring anyone to chase status updates.

Three regional rollout plans were designed, briefed, and approved in parallel — sequenced to account for market timing differences without creating dependency bottlenecks between regions.

The Turning Point

When the numbers started moving — and not all in the same direction.

About six weeks in, the EMEA numbers were tracking ahead of target. South America was moving but slower than projected. Europe was tracking exactly as planned. A different kind of campaign manager might have called this success and held the line. I treated it as information.

I realigned the media weight toward the channels that were converting in South America, adjusted the message cadence in that region based on what the EMEA data was telling us about audience response timing, and flagged the shift to leadership before it became visible in the monthly report. That transparency meant when the South America numbers caught up, it read as a strategic recovery — not a lucky turnaround.

"Three regions means three different versions of what is working. Reading them as one story is how you miss the real one."

What Came Of It

117% of target. Across all three regions.

117% Of the original campaign target achieved across EMEA, EU, and South America combined
4 months End-to-end delivery from brief to final results, on schedule across all markets
3 regions Unified under one campaign framework with locally adapted execution in each market

The campaign closed at 117 percent of the originally targeted numbers. More than the result, what I am proud of is that the outcome was not a surprise — it was the product of a strategy that was built to be readable in real time, adjusted as the data came in, and communicated clearly enough that leadership was never caught off guard by either the challenges or the wins.

A global campaign is not one campaign. It is one strategy with the discipline to show up differently in every room it enters.
