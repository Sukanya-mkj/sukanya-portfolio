GTM Workflow, AI-Augmented Operations

GTM Operations Lead · Claude, Microsoft Copilot, HubSpot, Asana · Fortune 500 Enterprise

The Situation

The team was capable. The overhead was killing their pace.

The GTM workflow was not broken in the way people usually mean when they say that. Campaigns were going out. Briefings were being written. Reporting was happening. But all of it was slower than it needed to be, and the slowness was not coming from people — it was coming from process overhead that no one had stopped to question.

BRDs that took three days to draft because they started from scratch every time. Status reports written manually from five different sources. Campaign briefs that required four rounds of editing to get to a usable state. I was brought in to run the GTM program and I came in looking at this thinking: none of this needs to be this slow.

Rebuilding the Engine

AI as an ops multiplier, not a content shortcut.

The first thing I was clear about was what AI was for. Not to generate campaign copy. Not to replace the judgment calls that still needed a human. The goal was to remove the mechanical work — the parts of the GTM workflow that consumed time without adding thinking — so the team could spend their hours on the work that actually required them.

I embedded Claude and Copilot into the BRD authoring process, built prompt frameworks that produced a working first draft from a structured intake form, and created a reporting template that pulled from live data sources instead of being assembled manually every week. HubSpot workflows were restructured so campaign status updated automatically at key handoff points rather than requiring someone to remember to log it.

Documentation turnaround dropped by roughly 40 percent. Five-plus hours of manual reporting per week were reclaimed across the team — redirected into strategic review and stakeholder communication instead.

The Harder Part

Getting the team to trust a process they did not build.

The tool changes were the easy part. The harder part was the team. People had been doing things a certain way for a long time, and introducing AI into the workflow raised real questions — not about whether the tools worked, but about what it meant for the work they were valued for. I spent time on that deliberately. I was clear about what the AI was not doing: it was not replacing judgment, it was not signing off on anything, and the quality bar had not moved.

"The question was never whether the AI could do it faster. The question was whether the team would trust a process that made their effort less visible."

Over time, they did. And once they saw that the reclaimed hours went toward work that was more interesting and higher impact, the resistance gave way to genuine adoption.

What Came Of It

Faster GTM. Sharper reporting. A team that kept the process.

~40% Reduction in documentation turnaround across BRDs, campaign briefs, and stakeholder reports
5+ hrs Per week reclaimed from manual reporting, redirected to strategic review and client communication
Full adoption The AI-augmented workflow became the team standard — not a pilot, not a workaround

The GTM campaigns ran on time. But the more lasting outcome was the process itself — a workflow the team owned, understood, and continued to run after the engagement closed. That is the version of AI implementation I am interested in: the one that outlasts the person who built it.

AI does not make a GTM workflow smarter. It makes the parts that were already smart move faster — so the humans in the room can spend their time on the parts that still require them.
