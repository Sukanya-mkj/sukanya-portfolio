Case 02

Retail Campaign Operations

PMO, transitioned from Copywriter · Adobe Workfront, Excel, PowerPoint · Retail Marketing Operations

The Situation

I came onto this retail account as a copywriter, at a point where campaign delivery was genuinely chaotic. Feedback loops were unclear, ownership between design and content kept shifting mid sprint, and nobody had drawn a clean line between what leadership was promising the client and what the teams on the ground could realistically deliver. Everyone involved was dealing with the same ambiguity, just from a different seat.

Stepping In

Moving from writing copy to managing the account meant earning trust I had not been formally given yet. The biggest challenge was understanding the gap between what leadership was asking the teams to do and what the teams delivering the work actually expected of the process, and finding a midpoint both sides could stand on.

I built sprint frameworks and delivery pipelines that translated leadership's expectations into something the delivery teams could realistically plan around, and translated the team's true capacity back up so leadership stopped overpromising to the client. Structuring campaign tracking this way cut the feedback delays that had been quietly stalling every cycle.

"People trusted me with account management before I had the title for it. I made sure I stood up to that trust every single time."
The Turning Point

The moment that tested me most was realizing people had started relying on me for judgment calls I had not been formally assigned to make. I leaned into it rather than stepping back from it. That meant leading with empathy for the delivery team's real workload, and leading with grace under pressure from the client side, because the fastest way to lose everyone's trust would have been to quietly let either side down.

What Came Of It

Campaign turnaround time dropped by 20 percent, measured across the full design to publish cycle, by restructuring the handoff points between design and content pods and running structured post-sprint retrospectives. The client stopped describing the work as simply on time and started describing it as consistently above expectation. The process itself went on to earn a Spot Award for delivery process innovation.

Leadership isn't assigned, it's claimed, when you see a system failing and decide to fix it.
